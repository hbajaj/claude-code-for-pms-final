What happened with 4.2, in plain words

  1. On 12 Aug, 4.2 cut the time a responder has to answer a ping from 90 to 60 seconds. It also lowered the weight on acceptance (0.40 → 0.25), and that never made the release notes.
  2. More pings ran out unanswered. The code scored a miss exactly like a "no" (−0.12), and scores never recovered on their own.
  3. Low score → fewer pings → no chance to earn points back. Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow and Meteor Mite all but vanished from rotation, and nobody, not them and not their handlers, could see why.
  4. A fix shipped on 24 Oct: the 90-second wait is back, misses are scored separately, scores recover, and the four were restored. The proposed 4.3 makes it visible, and the review-checklist skill catches the gaps in a brief before it ships.

Why it happened

A trap with no way out

Step 1

60 seconds to answer

Turn-downs still come at a median of ~22s, so misses are alerts noticed late, not slow decisions.

Step 2

A miss = a "no"

A miss cost −0.12, the same as turning a ping down. A yes earns only +0.08.

Step 3

Score sinks

No recovery, floor 0. The quiet four's replayed scores hit 0; everyone else sat at 0.72–1.0.

Step 4

Fewer pings

Routing asks the top-ranked first and stops at the first yes. A low rank means no pings.

Step 5

No way back

The only way to earn points is to take a ping, and those pings stopped coming.

The root cause: a miss was scored as a "no"

The prompt I wrote01-origin-story · prompt 7
How to fix this issue? What is the root cause? Vesper hardly being pinged (Aunt Dot) and Meteor Mite almost no callouts while The Gale is overloaded (Kip). Both are responders starved of pings after 4.2, and neither had a ticket before.

Led to routing-fix-proposal.md, the release runbook and score-reset-request.md. Approved by Helen and Wen; deployed 24 Oct 2026. All Module 1 prompts.

Root cause: a miss cost −0.12, the same as a turn-down, with no recovery and a floor of 0. The 60-second wait made misses common, so reliable people sank and never got the pings they needed to climb back.

Before · 4.2
Ping wait 60sA miss −0.12 (same as "no")Recovery noneQuiet four's scores → 0
After · 24 Oct fix
Ping wait 90sMisses scored separately; only 3+ in a row costRecovery +0.02 a dayRestored: Vesper 0.96, Meteor Mite 1.00, Farlight 0.84, The Undertow 0.88

Why restore them to their pre-4.2 scores and not to neutral (0.5)? At 0.5 they'd still rank below everyone else (0.72+), so they'd stay last.

4Power usedX-ray vision

The villain wasn't a person. It was one line of code.

The prompt I wrote04-x-ray-vision · prompt 7
Based on what you found, the reason some responders are getting no pings at all is ___, because ___. Fill the blanks for me and highlight in the same visual

Commit 6dcea32 (the code as it ran at 4.2): offer.py, history.py, config.py. All Module 4 prompts.

The reason some responders get no pings at all is a missed ping is scored as a turn-down and the score never recovers, because every answer that isn't a yes goes through record_declined (−0.12), and a responder at the bottom never gets a ping to earn the +0.08 back.

−0.12!
# config.py @ 6dcea32 OFFER_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 60 # was 90 until 4.2 WEIGHT_RECENT_ACCEPTANCE = 0.25 # was 0.40 until 4.2 # offer.py @ 6dcea32 for responder in routing.rank_for_callout(callout): answer = offer_to(responder, callout) if answer == TAKEN: history.record_accepted(responder) # +0.08 return responder history.record_declined(responder) # −0.12, misses too return None # history.py @ 6dcea32 # TODO(2019): should this ease back toward NEUTRAL_SCORE on its # own after a while? ... Leaving it as-is for now.

Excerpt from commit 6dcea32. The "+0.08" and "−0.12, misses too" notes are mine; the other comments are in the code.

Marcus's question, answered: 4.2 applied to everyone the same way. No responder joined after 12 Aug, and how often someone turned pings down before 4.2 didn't predict who sank. Vesper turned down only 15% before 4.2 and still lost about two-thirds of the pings.

How the sidekick keeps it from happening again

4.2's gaps are exactly what the checklist looks for

Each gap below is documented in this repo, and each is the kind of gap one of the four checks is built to flag before anything ships.

Every roadmap owner was Priya, who left on 21 Aug with no handover overlap, nine days after 4.2 shipped.
→
Owner namedFlags a missing owner, or one who has left.
The roadmap row said only "weight proximity more heavily", with no target and no guardrail. Nothing was set to flag out-of-area pings rising from 17% to 39%, or four responders going quiet.
→
Success measureNeeds a target and a guardrail, checked after launch.
A proximity change shipped together with a 90s → 60s ping wait and an acceptance-weight cut that never made the release notes.
→
Scope stays boundedFlags changes the opening ask didn't promise.
The roadmap stated the fix. The problem it was solving (a distant, high-acceptance responder picked over a nearby one) appeared only in Priya's handoff notes.
→
Problem before fixWho has the problem, and what goes wrong, comes first.

The validator makes each review trustworthy, and the review log stops a brief drifting out of sync after it's been checked.
